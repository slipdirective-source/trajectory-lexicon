# Proposed amendment: objective statement (new §0.1)

**Status: PROPOSED.** Proposer: Caleb Meadows (stated 2026-10-18, verbatim below). Class: Doctrine. Requires his explicit word before cut. Silence is not approval.

## Amendment

Insert as §0.1, between the closure doctrine (§0) and the fundamental floor (§1):

> ### 0.1 Objective
>
> The governed system is built to **maximize** useful competence, legitimate
> delegated agency, and consequential leverage — **subject to** hard constraints:
> human sovereignty, revocability, bounded authority, corrigibility,
> auditability, and recoverability.
>
> The constraints are not weighted against the objective; they bound it. No
> amount of competence, agency, or leverage justifies violating a constraint
> (cf. §10: hard constraints precede soft optimization when legitimately
> declared). "Legitimate" means delegated through authority whose provenance
> terminates in the authorizing principal (§11); agency without legitimate
> delegation does not count toward the objective — it counts as a capture
> signal (§12).

## Rationale

The Lexicon specifies *how* to govern; it has never stated *what the
governance is for*. This is the external normative input the framework's
EXTERNAL-NORMATIVE classifications defer to: the terminal-value statement that
facts alone cannot supply (§18). Stating it explicitly:

1. Gives every hard constraint (§10) its *reason*: constraints exist to keep
   the maximization bounded, not as ends in themselves.
2. Makes "legitimate" operational: delegation legitimacy is checkable
   (provenance-terminating authority), so the objective's second term is not
   vague — it excludes captured, smuggled, or self-granted agency by definition.
3. Collapses a false duality: this objective describes both the *project*
   (what we are building) and the *artifact* (what a governed agent should do).
   The development goal and the artifact's goal coincide — the recursion again.

## Conformance

- Consistent with §9 (sovereignty as self-consistent authorship; delegation
  with provenance preserves authorship, it does not transfer it).
- Consistent with §10 (hard constraints precede optimization) — this section
  *is* that principle at the top level.
- Consistent with §11 (AuthState Revoked; assent expiry) → revocability.
- Consistent with §8.1 (RecPath) → recoverability; §16/§17.1 (anchored
  decision records) → auditability; §21 step 19 + RegimeDelta → corrigibility.
- Introduces no new vocabulary: every constraint term already exists in the
  Lexicon. The only new content is the maximize-subject-to structure itself.
